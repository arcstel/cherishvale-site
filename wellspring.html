Section 01 · Executive Overview

Resolving the Data Center Water & Permitting Deadlock

A conventional 100 MW data center using evaporative cooling towers consumes 1 to 2 million gallons of potable municipal water per day during peak summer design conditions and adds 25–35% electrical overhead for chillers and fans during peak grid hours. Communities increasingly block permits over aquifer depletion, while moratoria cost cities billions in tax base and cost developers $12M–$25M per month in delayed compute deployment.

Core Architectural Principle — Strict Circuit Separation: To satisfy both utility engineers and environmental regulators, Wellspring enforces a hard boundary between (1) the closed facility cooling circuits (which never touch outside water or air), (2) the 100% capacity standby dry/sky heat-rejection loop (which guarantees full 100 MW cooling even when external heat recovery or canal loops are offline), and (3) the perimeter stormwater detention, treatment, and recharge facility (coupled only across double-wall sanitary isolation heat exchangers and fence-line electrical feeders).
Section 02 · Spatial & Civil Layout

The 4-Zone Campus & Subsurface Separation Concept

Rather than treating the mandatory 150–300 ft security setback as sterile turfgrass, Wellspring grades the campus into four functional zones from the elevated central compute pad outward to the regional watershed:

Zone 0 · Compute Core

100 MW IT Hall & CDU Mains

Four operating 25 MW cooling trains (plus a fifth N+1 redundant train) circulate closed-loop coolant through direct-to-chip cold plates and Coolant Distribution Units (CDUs). Zero evaporative cooling towers.

Zone 1 · Thermal Buffer

Thermal Storage & Standby Dry Coolers

Insulated Cold and Hot buffer tanks shift afternoon peak thermal loads to cooler night hours. Full-capacity fin-fan dry coolers and radiative sky panels provide 100% standalone heat rejection whenever external heat recovery is unavailable.

Zone 2 · Perimeter Berm

Stormwater Detention & Isolation HX

Terraced riprap bioswales and subsurface geocell crates detain 100-year storm surges. Across a double-wall isolation heat exchanger, recovered low-grade heat assists containerized membrane treatment and monitored EPA Class V recharge.

Zone 3 · Outbound Corridor

Stepped Aeration & Sub-Bed Loop

Treated water discharges down gravity-head vortex aeration basins with micro-hydro recovery, an assigned-duty closed PEX sub-bed winter biological warming circuit, and curtailed-DC water-quality polishing nodes.

Section 03 · Preliminary Engineering Basis (PE Review Reference)

Hydraulic Sizing, Approach Temperatures & Protection Basis

Assume 100 MW of thermal load (100 MWth) transferred into a liquid cooling system with water-like volumetric heat capacity (ρcp ≈ 4.184 MJ/m3·K) and a design temperature rise of ΔT = 10 °C (18 °F). Where “100 MW” denotes electrical service capacity or IT load, facility air-side cooling, pump work, and auxiliary loads must be reconciled separately in the heat balance.

V̇ = Q ρ cp ΔT = 100 MW (997 kg/m3)(4,184 J/kg·K)(10 K) ≈ 2.39 m3/s ≈ 37,900 US GPM
Recirculating Flow Note: 37,900 US GPM is closed-loop recirculating flow, not freshwater consumption. At a 5 °C rise, required flow doubles to 75,800 GPM; at a 15 °C rise (or with latent phase-change augmentation), flow decreases to ≤25,300 GPM.

Graphene Mass Balance (Why Feedstock Cost Is Negligible)

Monolayer graphene has a theoretical specific surface area of 2,630 m2/g. At a 0.2 wt% (2 g/L) suspension loading, the raw natural flake graphite ($2,000/metric ton industrial grade) required across all three project scales is minimal:

Phase 1 Benchtop Skid (100 L)

Requires 200 grams of exfoliated graphene (<0.5 lb). Raw flake graphite feedstock cost: ~$1.00.

Phase 2 Pilot Membranes (100 GPM)

Ultrathin (200 nm) Graphene-Oxide (GO) selective layers require ~1 g/m2—under 1 kg of GO for an entire 40-ft pilot container skid.

100 MW Full Thermal Loop (4,000 m3)

Requires 8.0 Metric Tons of graphene to charge the entire 1-million-gallon thermal storage ring. Raw flake graphite feedstock: ~$16,000 total.

Section 06 · Watershed & Canal Engineering

Outbound Treatment Corridor & Thermal Duty Boundaries

Where treated stormwater or greywater is discharged to a surface waterway under an NPDES permit, the outbound channel is engineered as a stepped aeration and biological polishing corridor—subject to strict thermal and hydraulic limits:

Assigned Canal Thermal Duty (Winter Only)

The closed PEX sub-bed heating circuit beneath the lined biological denitrification zone is sized for an assigned fractional duty (e.g., 1 to 5 MWth during winter months) to keep root-zone microbes at 20–22 °C when ambient water falls below 10 °C. It is never relied upon as the primary 100 MW heat sink, and automatically isolates in summer so receiving-stream temperature limits are strictly respected.

Stepped Vortex Aeration & Micro-Hydro

Gravity drop from the elevated campus pad drives hyperbolic weir cascades that mechanically raise Dissolved Oxygen (DO > 8.5 mg/L) while low-head vortex turbines harvest 5–25 kW of local auxiliary power for turbidity/DO telemetry and UV-C disinfection banks.

Curtailed-Power Electro-Remediation

When on-site solar/wind PPAs curtail or AI workloads trough, a low-voltage DC feeder in the canal bank powers submerged electrocoagulation plates (precipitating phosphorus and suspended solids into cleanout sumps) and nanobubble generators that suppress downstream algal blooms.

Section 08 · Policy Framework & Staged Pilot Roadmap

The “Water-Positive Compute” Municipal Ordinance & Containerized Pilot

A city council, county commission, or state legislature can adopt the Wellspring standard without creating a new regulatory agency by establishing a Conditioned Zoning Fast-Track paired with a Co-Located Fence-Line Municipal Easement:

1. Expedited Zoning for Zero-Potable-Draw Facilities

Any data center ≥10 MW engineered for (a) zero net draw of municipal potable water for cooling, (b) 100% standalone non-evaporative summer heat-rejection standby, and (c) 100-year on-site stormwater detention receives a 6-month expedited site-plan and building-permit review.

2. Fence-Line Thermal & Electrical Easement

The data center operator does not become a regulated public water utility. Instead, the operator provides a dedicated pad at the perimeter fence supplied with isolated warm water (via double-wall exchanger HX-301) and curtailed DC power. The local Water District or its contracted operator owns and runs the treatment skid under existing NPDES / EPA Class V permits.

3. Federal & State Resilience Grant Stacking

Because the perimeter detention basin, treatment skid, and outbound aeration canal are public watershed assets within the municipal easement, they qualify for EPA Clean Water State Revolving Fund (CWSRF) low-interest financing, FEMA BRIC pre-disaster flood mitigation grants, and IIJA PFAS/emerging-contaminant remediation funds.

4. Staged Verification Before Full-Scale Reliance

The ordinance requires a 100 GPM containerized pilot (“Kidney Pod”) to verify local source-water fouling, membrane flux, reject disposal, and hydrogeological recharge acceptance before full watershed credits are certified.

3-Phase De-Risking Roadmap

Phase 1 — 10 kW Benchtop Loop ($75k–$150k, 90 Days): University or municipal lab test loop measuring SpringCore-48 latent heat capacity, pump curve / viscosity behavior, and copper/elastomer coupon compatibility.
Phase 2 — 100 GPM Containerized Water-Treatment Pilot ($850k–$1.2M, 6 Months): Co-located 40-ft skid plumbed to an existing facility’s isolated heat-recovery exchanger and stormwater basin to log real-world membrane recovery, CIP frequency, brine mass balance, and EPA Class V recharge water quality.
Phase 3 — 100 MW Reference Campus Deployment: Full integration of the 4+1 N+1 DN600 closed cooling trains, diurnal thermal buffer tanks, and perimeter flood-detention easement.
