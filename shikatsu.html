Learn a character properly, not just recognise it

Most apps flash a card and ask you to pick the meaning. Shikatsu teaches the thing you actually need to write by hand — the order, direction and shape of every stroke — and then makes you do it. Browse the whole kana chart and tap any character to hear it, watch it and write it.

Watch it drawn

Each stroke animates on in the correct order — numbered, with a bright arrow showing which way the pen travels. The full character sits faintly behind as a guide.

Trace it with a pen

Draw on the grid with a stylus or your finger. Shikatsu recognises each stroke and checks it against the real thing — wrong strokes flash red, and it hints the right one if you get stuck.

Practise what's weak

Your drills quietly favour the characters you keep missing, so time goes where it's needed. Streaks and accuracy are tracked on your device.

How it works

Watch

The character draws itself stroke by stroke, arrow by arrow — the way a teacher would write it on the board.

Trace

Draw it yourself. Every stroke is graded in order, so you can't accidentally learn it backwards.

Remember

Switch to the typing and reading drills to lock the sound, the kana and the keystrokes together.

Three ways to drill the same character

Write

Stroke order, direction arrows and guided tracing with a pen or finger — the full handwriting workout.

Type

See the reading, build the kana. Shikatsu converts as you type, so “kya” becomes きゃ and you learn the keystrokes by doing.

Read

See a kana, type its romaji. A quick, keyboard-friendly drill for recognition and speed.

Japanese now — more scripts on the way

Shikatsu starts with the full hiragana and katakana sets, including dakuten and yōon combinations. The same engine is built for writing systems generally, so Chinese, Korean, Arabic and more can follow as new decks — the drills, the tracing and the scoring all stay the same.

  • Hiragana 五十音 and 全部, with dakuten and handakuten
  • Yōon combinations like きゃ, しゅ, ちょ
  • Katakana, including everyday loanwords
  • Read, Type and Write drills over any deck

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription, no account. It's part of the Cherishvale studio's set of calm, offline apps.

Do I need a Japanese keyboard?

No. The typing drills work with the on-screen romaji keyboard (or a physical one) and convert what you type into kana, so you learn the keystrokes without configuring an IME.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Shikatsu is an installable web app — add it to your home screen and it keeps working with no connection. All the stroke data ships with it.

Will it work with my stylus?

Yes. It uses standard pointer input, so an Apple Pencil, an S Pen or any stylus on a tablet or phone works — as does a finger.

How does it check my handwriting?

Each character ships as its real strokes and stroke centre-lines, from the open KanjiVG and animCJK data. As you draw, Shikatsu matches your stroke against the expected one and grades it in order.

What does the name mean?

死活 (shikatsu) is a term from the game of Go — “life and death”. Here it means learning a writing system so well it becomes second nature.

On privacy

Shikatsu runs entirely in your browser. There is no account, no analytics and nothing sent anywhere — your practice stays on your device. Stroke data comes from the open KanjiVG and animCJK projects; animation and handwriting recognition use the open-source Hanzi Writer.
