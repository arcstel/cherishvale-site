1. The ancestral land description

The Tresimond, Doris, Lovett and Duhon instruments repeatedly describe approximately 300 acres, Block or Subdivision No. 3, from Treville Granger’s estate, within a 900-acre tract in the William Dyson League, Orange County, Texas. The Duhon deed also refers to a subdivision of 1,500 acres.

  • The 300 acres are described as set apart to Erisse Granger Corbello in her father’s estate partition.
  • William Morgan is named as county surveyor for that partition.
  • The historical grant to William Dyson is described as dated February 13, 1835, by Coahuila and Texas.
  • Later records identify the Dyson survey as Abstract No. 10 / A-10.

These are useful matching keys. A lease or deed elsewhere in the same league does not automatically concern this 300-acre tract.

A Louisiana opinion documents historical oil revenues

Corbello v. Corbello, 171 La. 735, 132 So. 127, Louisiana Supreme Court; opinion December 1, 1930, rehearing denied January 5, 1931. The other window located a case PDF on the Samsung USB. Its OCR was independently read for this page.

  • The opinion describes Mrs. Erisse Corbello obtaining valuable Orange County oil property in 1924, being interdicted in March 1925, and Calcasieu National Bank of Southwest Louisiana serving as curator.
  • Reports her death on February 3, 1930.
  • Reports $24,388 in royalties and bonuses, and $22,645.95 in expenditures during the curatorship.
  • Separately describes $3,500 initial capital received from a Texas guardian; at death, $5,243.04 cash subject to $828.70 charges, or $4,414.34 net, besides a residence and Orange County oil properties.
  • Reports $820 in advances to some appellants. This does not identify all eventual heirs or establish distributions to this user’s branch.
  • The bank corrected a $350 commission charge; the judgment also credited the estate $72.50 interest. The judgment approving the account as corrected was affirmed.

Why it matters: this is a court-reported history of actual oil revenue and estate administration, rather than a later lease’s payment clause. It does not itself establish that this Erisse is identical to every similarly named person in the later affidavits, determine current title, or show a government reparations award.

11. Late-evening court & archival findings

Book 41 pp. 210–212 now retrieved

The other research window downloaded these pages from Orange County’s Imaged Books collection; this window read the page images directly. The official record is Erisse Carbello et al. v. Abe Sokolski et al., Equity No. 234, U.S. District Court, Eastern District of Texas at Beaumont. This is not the original Granger estate partition.

  1. November 28, 1921: certified order pro confesso against named defendants; recorded starting on page 210.
  2. December 30, 1921: the later decree recites that a final decree was entered, recorded in federal minutes December 31, awarding complainants the relief prayed for against named defendants. That earlier final decree’s complete text is still needed.
  3. February 14, 1925: Judge W. L. Estes orders the 1921 final decree to “stand and remain in full force and effect,” unaffected by dismissal of the remaining defendants. Those remaining defendants are dismissed without prejudice to complainants’ rights against them.
  4. May 9, 1925: federal clerk J. R. Blades, by deputy Susie Kerr, certifies the copies. Filed in Orange County May 13 and recorded May 23, 1925.

The 1925 decree recites that Erisse Carbello, by next friend John L. Wasey, joined pro forma by husband Charles Carbello, and John L. Wasey individually, alleged fee-simple ownership of the described 300-acre Block 3. It explicitly connects the tract to Erisse’s portion of father Treville Granger’s partition and county surveyor William Morgan.

Meaning for the claim: this is contemporaneous court-record evidence of relief granted against specific defendants. It is stronger than a later assertion of ownership. It does not establish a present-day share, bind unnamed successors automatically, or show that Erisse alone owned every interest: Wasey was also an individual complainant. Obtain his interest documents, the original complaint, the full 1921 decree and subsequent conveyances.

Granger settlement payments described in a published opinion

This window read the full Gulf Production Co. v. Granger opinion and rehearing. It identifies Mose Granger et al. v. Gulf Production Company et al., Orange County District Court No. 4397, and describes:

  • A January 1922 settlement with Humble for cash plus a stated 2% oil-payment interest on a disputed 10⅔-acre strip.
  • A January 19, 1922 settlement with Gulf and its lessors for $10,000 cash plus 1/96 of oil on specified lease lands excluding that strip; a conditional 1/40 oil interest concerned the strip.
  • The court rejected the argument that the limited Gulf/Humble compromise alone triggered the contract’s suit-settlement exception. On rehearing, it expressly did not decide liability under another contract provision; this should not be summarized as a blanket award of ongoing royalties.
  • Named beneficiaries/participants include Mose Granger, Mary Scales, Millie Caron, Sarah Jane Granger, Ada Granger, and specified minors, with assignments to John B. Warren and J. A. Conn discussed.

The disputed strip ultimately was held to be in the James Dyson League; the litigation involved the boundary with the William Dyson League. These contractual payment interests are a separate lead, not established payments on Erisse’s Block 3 or proof that this family is a beneficiary. Obtain No. 4397 and the original contracts before making that connection.

Boundary history read directly

Chesson v. La Flore describes Treville Granger Sr.’s five heirs and a northern 300-acre allotment to Treville Granger Jr., subsequently split into 60-acre tracts. That northern allotment must not be conflated with Erisse’s Block 3 solely because both are 300 acres. The decision reversed and rendered a five-acre title dispute in favor of the appellant; it did not award this family the whole league.

12. “Heirs and assigns forever” — exact clauses

The family specifically identified perpetual-inheritance wording as important. The reviewed documents contain it in several places; it should be preserved as part of the evidence rather than omitted.

Duhon trustee deed · Vol. 624 p. 211

“TO HAVE AND HOLD the said premises, together with all and singular the rights, privileges and appurtenances thereto in any manner belonging unto the said Gary W. Duhon, Trustee for the heirs of Erisse Granger Corbello, their heirs and assigns, forever…”

The clause continues that neither the corporation nor persons claiming under it shall thereafter claim an interest in the premises. This explicitly identifies heirs of Erisse as the trustee’s beneficiary description. Identifying those beneficiaries, the grantor’s actual title and subsequent transfers remains essential.

Other occurrences

  • 1988 LeBleu donation, Vol. 689 pp. 118–121: describes property and the donors’ rights, titles and interests passing to the named donees and “unto their heirs and assigns forever.” Its 8.5-acre and 4.725-acre tracts require separate boundary matching.
  • 1994 deeds, Vol. 909 pp. 267, 554–555 and 613–614: use “heirs and assigns forever” and warranty language for described 2.5-acre tracts. Their existence does not establish that all 300 acres passed by those deeds.
  • 2001 affidavit/deed, Vol. 1248 p. 890: contains the wording “their heirs and assigned for ever” with irregular spelling. Its title and heirship assertions require the reconciliation described above.

Research interpretation: “heirs and assigns forever” generally signals an enduring, inheritable and transferable interest, subject to the rest of the instrument and applicable law. “Assigns” includes later transferees. It does not alone guarantee that every future descendant retains a share despite later conveyances, reservations or court orders, nor does “forever warrant” promise perpetual royalty checks. The strongest evidence is the full granting and reservation language, the grantor’s title, the beneficiary/inheritance chain and the later record history.

Sources read: Joseph Lovett/Duhon - William Dyson Descendants.PDF, PDF page 2; Documents with Forever/Forever_1.PDF, donation clause; the 1994 deeds and Corbello_1.PDF identified above. No present ownership fraction is calculated from this wording alone.
