1. The ancestral land description

The Tresimond, Doris, Lovett and Duhon instruments repeatedly describe approximately 300 acres, Block or Subdivision No. 3, from Treville Granger’s estate, within a 900-acre tract in the William Dyson League, Orange County, Texas. The Duhon deed also refers to a subdivision of 1,500 acres.

  • The 300 acres are described as set apart to Erisse Granger Corbello in her father’s estate partition.
  • William Morgan is named as county surveyor for that partition.
  • The historical grant to William Dyson is described as dated February 13, 1835, by Coahuila and Texas.
  • Later records identify the Dyson survey as Abstract No. 10 / A-10.

These are useful matching keys. A lease or deed elsewhere in the same league does not automatically concern this 300-acre tract.

Exhibit A: Spanish Archives

An old Spanish-language land document accompanies a General Land Office certification dated June 17, 1986, identifying Volume 22, pages 303–305, Spanish Archives. A clearer official copy is needed for reliable transcription and translation. The microfilm certificate also appears to contain a year inconsistent with the execution and filing dates.

Exhibit B: examiner’s opinion

PDF page 5 / recorded page 214 contains an August 7, 1985 letter from J. Frank Stubbs, questioned-document examiner, to Mrs. Betty Cayley. He expresses the opinion that historical signatures or marks attributed to William Dyson, John A. Veatch and others were written by different named persons. PDF page 6 contains comparison measurements.

This establishes that an examiner raised and documented those concerns. It is not itself a court judgment establishing forgery.

The later family affidavit names Marguerite Erisse Granger Corbello et al. v. Abe Sokolski et al., in the U.S. District Court, Eastern District of Texas, Beaumont, and cites Volume 41, pages 210–212. The original court record has not yet been retrieved. The defendant’s surname and other caption spellings should be checked against the original.

The 1987 recorded claim

The handwritten instrument labeled “Special Warranty Deed,” No. 55660, was filed November 19, 1987, at Volume 672, pages 289–291. It asserts Corbello-heir possession beginning in June 1983 of 300 acres, Block 3, and cites Book 41 page 211 and “Federal Court Ruling Equity #234.” It associates the older record with 1925; that is a later claimant’s reference, not independently verified case dating.

Robert L. Corbello records a claim to an undivided inherited interest. The clerk’s memo notes unsatisfactory legibility. This is a recorded claim, not the federal judgment, and it contains no payment ledger.

Local sources: Joseph Lovett/Curious_Document.PDF and PDF27751.PDF, visually reproducing the same instrument. The other window also found a matching Curious_Document.PDF on the Samsung USB archive.

A Louisiana opinion documents historical oil revenues

Corbello v. Corbello, 171 La. 735, 132 So. 127, Louisiana Supreme Court; opinion December 1, 1930, rehearing denied January 5, 1931. The other window located a case PDF on the Samsung USB. Its OCR was independently read for this page.

  • The opinion describes Mrs. Erisse Corbello obtaining valuable Orange County oil property in 1924, being interdicted in March 1925, and Calcasieu National Bank of Southwest Louisiana serving as curator.
  • Reports her death on February 3, 1930.
  • Reports $24,388 in royalties and bonuses, and $22,645.95 in expenditures during the curatorship.
  • Separately describes $3,500 initial capital received from a Texas guardian; at death, $5,243.04 cash subject to $828.70 charges, or $4,414.34 net, besides a residence and Orange County oil properties.
  • Reports $820 in advances to some appellants. This does not identify all eventual heirs or establish distributions to this user’s branch.
  • The bank corrected a $350 commission charge; the judgment also credited the estate $72.50 interest. The judgment approving the account as corrected was affirmed.

Why it matters: this is a court-reported history of actual oil revenue and estate administration, rather than a later lease’s payment clause. It does not itself establish that this Erisse is identical to every similarly named person in the later affidavits, determine current title, or show a government reparations award.

Source: Corbello v. Corbello; local USB case PDF and /tmp/opencode/famdocs/corbello_v_corbello.txt, PDF pages 1–4.

Other reported court leads

  • Chesson v. La Flore, 191 S.W. 745 (Tex. Civ. App. 1916): other-window report describes Treville Granger Sr.’s death in 1887, approximately northern 1,500 acres of the William Dyson Survey, five heirs and a partition. Obtain and verify the full opinion and underlying partition.
  • Gulf Production Co. v. Granger, 57 S.W.2d 1116 (Tex. 1932): reported litigation involving Granger heirs and Dyson League mineral/boundary rights; tract and party matching remain necessary.
  • Dyson Descendant Corp. v. Sonat Exploration Co., 861 S.W.2d 942 (Tex. App. 1993): concerns the James Dyson League, not the William Dyson League. It is not a direct title determination for this 300-acre tract.

8. Priority record searches

  1. Original Equity No. 234: obtain the exact caption, docket, pleadings, decree, exhibits, appeals and any payment/distribution orders from the federal court or archival custodian. Retrieve Vol. 41 pp. 210–212 and confirm the county record series.
  2. Duhon trusteeship: follow Vol. 624 pp. 211–215, all deeds to/from Gary W. Duhon as trustee, beneficiary lists, trust agreement, accounting and distributions.
  3. Original estate partition: Treville Granger’s probate/partition, William Morgan survey and Block 3 boundaries. Also retrieve the 1959 decree, Vol. 235 p. 542.
  4. Reconcile identity and heirship: compare the 1986 affidavits, 2001 affidavit, Vol. 627 p. 962 / instrument 41237, the Louisiana curatorship/succession, and birth, marriage and death records. Establish each generation of the Hargrove/Hagar/Corbello chain and Juan’s place in it.
  5. Official lease verification: use RRC 03-03243 and the API identifiers to retrieve lease maps, well history, operator history and production; identify the official OCAD mineral account. Match the footprint to Block 3.
  6. Trace actual money: division orders, operator title requirements, suspense-account correspondence, royalty statements, curator/succession accounts, settlement and distribution orders. Check Texas unclaimed property only after identities are established.
  7. Complete the archive review: remaining Bank PDFs and images, distinguishing unrelated surface deeds and same-name people from the claim tract.

Historical royalty receipts, present production estimates, appraised mineral interests and compensation awards are different kinds of evidence. This notebook does not calculate a family entitlement or promise recovery.
