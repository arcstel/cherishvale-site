PLANETARY EXPLORER

An offline 3D atlas of the Solar System — 20 worlds, a live orbit map for any date between 1800 and 2050, a quarter of a million place names, and street-level maps of Earth.

From the whole system down to a single street

The live orbit map. Every planet where it really is, for any date from 1800 to 2050 — set a date, press play, watch the system turn. Track named comets, switch the orbit drawing style, or flip to true scale to feel how empty space really is. 244,625 place names. Earth carries a full gazetteer that reveals itself as you descend — countries, states and provinces, capitals, cities, peaks, seas, lakes, rivers. Filter by category, or search and fly to anywhere. Street-level Earth. Open any of seven metro areas as a flat, pannable map at about 1.2 metres per pixel, now stored as tile pyramids so streets stay sharp as you zoom — individual streets and buildings, from public-domain aerial imagery.

For sky watchers and astrologers

A full birth-chart engine. Enter a date, time, UTC offset and place to get the Ascendant, Midheaven and twelve house cusps in Placidus, whole sign, equal or Porphyry — plus every planet with sign, degree and retrograde state, a complete aspect list, an aspect grid and a house table. Calculated offline from our own ephemeris. Choose your zodiac: tropical, sidereal with five ayanamsas, Vedic nakshatras, or Chinese year animal and solar terms. Synastry. Add a second person and both charts share one bi-wheel — inner ring and outer ring — with the cross-aspects drawn between them, a full inter-aspect list and a synastry grid. Classic 2D chart. Prefer the traditional look? One switch redraws the wheel flat and print-like — element-colored sign band, glyphs, every cusp degree, planet degree ticks and a center data block — with synastry, transits, progressions and solar returns carried over. Moon mode. The Moon with its real surface and true phase for any date: illumination, phase angle, moon age, distance, angular size, the next four phases, rise/transit/set for your location, and a day-by-day phase calendar for the month.

The whole sky, drawn

41,411 stars (108,000 optional). 88 constellations. 1,900 deep-sky objects. A real chart of the night sky — every constellation with its stick figure and IAU boundary, thousands of stars, and the Messier, Caldwell and brightest NGC/IC galaxies, nebulae and clusters, plotted where they are and coloured by type. Filter the list, tap an object, and read a Tonight plan: its altitude now, transit time and height, rise and set, and a 24-hour altitude curve. Toggle 🔴 Night mode to dim the whole app to red and keep your dark adaptation. Jupiter's moons and Saturn's rings. The same panel shows where Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto are right now, and how far Saturn's rings are tilted open, with Titan alongside — computed from the real orbital periods and each planet's axial tilt. Your own observing lists. Star any object to save it, then work from Favourites, or from built-in Messier, Caldwell and binocular-best lists — each row showing its live altitude and bearing so you know what is worth looking at tonight. Saved on the device. Setup & Help. A built-in guide walks you through connecting an ASCOM Alpaca or INDI mount and setting up AR, including how to nudge the AR heading and field of view if your device's compass drifts. No manual required. First Light. One tap before you go out: a readiness checklist (time and place, telescope, AR camera and motion, night mode), tonight's darkness window, the Moon's phase and position, what is up right now with altitude and bearing, and the month's coming events.

Rise, set and the seasons

The Almanac. A month at a glance for your location — sunrise and sunset, civil twilight, moonrise and moonset, day length and the moon's phase for every day, with the equinoxes and solstices marked and the traditional name of the month's full moon. Sky events, all month. Below the calendar is everything worth looking up for: solar and lunar eclipses, planet oppositions, solar conjunctions and greatest elongations, close planet conjunctions, and the meteor showers peaking that month. Computed offline from our own ephemeris.

Space weather, projected

The Sun, as it is now. ☀ Flares mode plots the Sun's active regions from public-domain NOAA SWPC data — location, size, sunspot class and C-flare / M-flare / X-flare probabilities. Our own projection geometry rotates each region to the date you choose, draws it on the Earth-facing disk, and measures the angle to the Sun–Earth line, so you can see which regions are Earth-directed and whether a CME's cone would reach us. Flare history, planetary Kp, solar-wind speed and density, the interplanetary field's Bz and the sunspot number are all in the panels. It works fully offline from a bundled snapshot; if you like, you can let it refresh straight from NOAA when you have a connection.

A codex for every world

The Codex. Each world opens as a historical page — a parchment that unfurls around the planet as it turns slowly on its dais, with the ancient symbol as a watermark and sections on naming, history, key facts and the name in other languages. Browse and search all 340+ catalogued bodies: the Sun, the planets, dwarf planets, 191 named moons, notable comets and asteroids, and a curated set of notable exoplanets from other star systems.

Public-domain data, bundled offline. Weather, explained. An optional classroom schematic of the semi-permanent highs and lows: idealized isobars and circulating surface winds, with the Coriolis sense made explicit. Clearly labelled a teaching model, not live data. Other worlds. Over 100 notable exoplanets from the NASA Exoplanet Archive — Proxima b, the TRAPPIST-1 family, the hottest and puffiest planets, and the historic firsts — each with a Codex page, searchable by planet or host-star name. Teacher mode. Guided lessons at three levels (Earth Science, High School, College), an offline quiz, and printable worksheets — built for projectors, tablets and no Wi-Fi.

What it does

20 worlds

Mercury to Neptune, plus Pluto, Charon, the Moon, Phobos, Deimos, Ceres, Vesta, Europa, Ganymede, Titan, Enceladus and Bennu — each with its own globe, data panel and named features.

Live orbit map

A true Solar-System map with planets on their real orbits for any date from 1800 to 2050. Play the clock, jump to presets, and follow named comets including Halley, Hale-Bopp, NEOWISE and the interstellar visitors ʻOumuamua, Borisov and 3I/ATLAS.

244,625 place names

Earth's full gazetteer — countries, states, capitals, cities and physical features — revealed in tiers as you zoom. Filter by category or search any name and fly to it.

Tiled deep detail

Earth gains a high-resolution layer (~1.6 km per pixel) blended in as you approach, held in memory one tile at a time.

Street-level Earth

Seven metro areas open as flat, pannable street maps at ~1.2 m per pixel, from public-domain aerial imagery — close enough to pick out individual buildings.

Earth layers

One 🖼 Layers panel gathers the real data overlays — a NASA total-column ozone map with a Dobson-Unit legend, and geology (plate boundaries, major faults and 1,571 volcanoes), plus a label backdrop for readability. All public-domain data, all offline.

Weather-teaching layer

An optional classroom schematic of the semi-permanent highs and lows — idealized isobars and circulating surface winds, with the Coriolis sense made explicit. Clearly labelled a teaching model, not live data.

Exoplanets

Over 100 notable worlds from other star systems, curated from the NASA Exoplanet Archive — Proxima b, the TRAPPIST-1 family, the hottest and puffiest planets and the historic firsts. Browse them in the Codex and search by planet or host-star name.

Smooth, natural zoom

Zoom follows altitude, so the ground tracks your finger from whole planet down to a city. Scroll, pinch, or drag the slider; labels resolve continent → country → state → city.

Astrology mode

A complete birth-chart engine — Ascendant, Midheaven and houses in Placidus, whole sign, equal or Porphyry, with positions, retrograde states, aspects, an aspect grid and a house table, plus element/modality balance, essential dignities, the lunar nodes and Chiron, aspect patterns, retrograde stations, fixed-star contacts, midpoints, the Arabic parts, transits, progressions and the solar return. A Classic 2D style redraws the wheel in the traditional flat idiom for working astrologers.

Every mode, one tap

A Modes grid jumps to any view — atlas, solar system, sky tracker, space weather, astrology, Moon, almanac, AR, codex, First light — each with a one-line how-to and its own help.

Synastry

Compare two people on a single bi-wheel: both charts, every cross-aspect, an inter-aspect list and a synastry grid.

Moon mode

The Moon's true phase for any date — illumination, age, distance, angular size, next phases, rise/set times and a month of phases at a glance.

Full sky chart

41,411 stars (108,000 optional), all 88 constellations with figures and IAU boundaries, and 1,900 deep-sky objects — Messier, Caldwell and bright NGC/IC — plotted and searchable, with a Tonight planner and public-domain photographs of famous objects.

Teacher mode

A classroom layer: guided lessons at three levels (Earth Science, High School, College), an offline quiz, and printable worksheets. Built for projectors, tablets and no Wi-Fi.

Ambient music

An optional soft ambient tone, generated for the app and bundled offline — no streaming, no licence, no ads. Toggle it from the bar.

Made for every screen

A layout that adapts from phone to tablet and landscape — the toolbar tidies itself to icons on small phones — with keyboard focus, screen-reader labels and reduced-motion support.

Jupiter & Saturn

Jupiter's four Galilean moons positioned from their real orbital periods — with transits, occultations and shadow transits marked — and Saturn's ring opening angle with Titan, all live as you change the date.

Observing lists

Star your favourites, or browse built-in Messier, Caldwell and binocular-best lists with each object's live altitude and bearing. Create your own named lists, reorder them, and import or export as text.

Telescope control

Connect to an ASCOM Alpaca mount on your own network and GoTo or Sync the selected object, with abort and park. No account, no cloud.

AR overlay

Point your camera at the sky and the chart is drawn over it — stars, constellations, planets and deep-sky objects, aligned to your device. Falls back to drag-to-look without a compass.

Setup & Help built in

A step-by-step guide for connecting a telescope and setting up AR, with on-screen alignment controls if your device's compass or field of view needs a nudge.

First Light

A one-tap pre-session checklist: darkness window, Moon, what is up now with bearings, telescope/AR readiness, and the month's events.

Share & export

Send tonight's plan or any observing list to a friend, the clipboard, or a text file — with coordinates, magnitudes and bearings.

41,411 stars (108,000 optional)

A deep naked-eye and binocular star catalogue to magnitude 8, with all 88 constellations, and 1,900 deep-sky objects on top.

Night & sepia modes

Tap once to dim the whole interface to red for dark-adapted observing, or switch to a warm sepia tone for comfortable daytime and low-blue reading.

Natural, precise touch

Drag follows your finger at every scale — from whole planet down to a single city — with sensitivity that eases off as you zoom in, so the ground never whips past.

Almanac

A month calendar with sunrise, sunset, twilight, moonrise, moonset, day length, daily moon phase, the season dates and the full moon's traditional name.

Sky events

Each month's eclipses, planet oppositions, conjunctions and greatest elongations, and the meteor showers peaking that month — computed offline.

Solar-flare projection

The Sun's active regions from public-domain NOAA SWPC data, with our own geometry for flare risk and Earth impact, plus flare history, Kp and the solar wind — all offline.

340+-body codex

A historical page for every catalogued body — naming, etymology, discovery and key facts — browsable and searchable, now including notable exoplanets. Worlds with imagery open as 3D globes.

Fully offline

Every texture, tile and label is bundled in the app. No internet needed, no ads, no accounts, no tracking. (You can optionally let the space-weather view refresh from NOAA.)

Guided welcome

A short first-run tour introduces the worlds, the system bar and the sky tools, so a new explorer is never lost. Reopen it any time from About.

True 360° globes

Spin every world freely in any direction — no pole lock — with altitude-aware drag so the ground tracks your finger at any zoom.

Real imagery

NASA / USGS spacecraft data and public-domain aerial photography, with original procedurally-generated gas-giant artwork.

Toward real

Axial tilts, polar flattening and rings for the gas giants, and adjustable orbit-path styling.

Open data & licences

Planetary Explorer is built on open and public-domain data. The app itself is proprietary; the datasets below remain under their own licences and are bundled as derived works.

NASA · USGS · ESA · NOAA imagery

Public domain (U.S. Government works and agency releases). Surface imagery for the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Venus, Earth and more, plus the space-weather data. Used with attribution as a courtesy.

OpenNGC — deep-sky catalogue

CC BY-SA 4.0 · mattiaverga/OpenNGC. Every Messier and Caldwell object plus bright NGC/IC clusters, nebulae and galaxies, bundled as a derived dataset under the same licence.

HYG Database v3 — deep stars

CC BY-SA 4.0 · astronexus/HYG-Database. The optional 108,000-star catalogue to magnitude 10.

d3-celestial — stars & constellations

BSD-3-Clause · ofrohn/d3-celestial. Star positions from the Hipparcos catalogue; constellation figures and IAU boundaries.

GeoNames · Natural Earth — place names

GeoNames, CC BY 4.0; countries and regions from Natural Earth (public domain). The 244,000-name Earth gazetteer.

NOAA SWPC · IMO/IAU — space weather & showers

Public domain. Solar active regions, X-ray flares, Kp and solar wind; and the annual meteor-shower facts.

Ambient music

Generated for this app. No third-party recordings.
