Authentication photo checklist

Consolidated from the “verification to request” notes on every item page. Photographing these will let a specialist move much faster — and is exactly what an Antiques Roadshow appraiser will ask for if the objects cannot be examined in person. Shoot in bright, even light; get close; keep the object still.

Every ceramic, glass, and stoneware piece

  • The entire base/underside, straight-on and in sharp focus — this is where marks live.
  • Any stamp, seal, backstamp, or painted mark, filling the frame.
  • Height and maximum diameter (a ruler or tape in the shot helps).
  • The interior, plus close-ups of chips, hairlines, or repairs.
  • Items 22, 27, 28: read the seal/reign marks — a Chinese-ceramics specialist can often identify these from a clear photo.
  • Item 21: the underside of each candlestick base (look for pontil scarring or an etched mark) and the height.

Prints, paintings, and framed works (items 1, 6, 7, 16, 17, 18)

  • The full lower margin / caption / signature, shot straight-on without glare.
  • The back of the frame: any labels, stencils, gallery stickers, or handwritten notes.
  • What the image is printed on — paper, canvas-textured paper, or board (visible at an edge).
  • Item 17 specifically: an unobstructed, full-frontal photo of the whole portrait plus the reverse. This is the collection's top-value unknown.
  • Item 1: the entire bottom edge looking for Currier & Ives / Nassau Street publisher text.

Silver, plate, and metalwork (items 3, 4, 15, 19, 26)

  • Every hallmark or stamp: “STERLING,” “925,” “800,” “EPNS,” “A1,” “plate,” or a maker's mark.
  • Weights where practical — especially the tray (4) and teapot (26).
  • A simple magnet test noted per piece (magnet sticks = not sterling).
  • Do not polish before photographing — tarnish can hide marks, and polishing can reduce value.
  • Item 15: photograph each jewelry piece individually — front, back, clasp, and any mark — one at a time over a padded surface.

Wood and furniture (items 8, 9)

  • The underside and any joints, labels, or stencils.
  • Item 8: a close-up of the “OIL SALEM” stencil on the seat rail, and whether the chair rocks steadily.
  • Item 9: how the lid fits/opens, the interior, and the underside of both the container and the carved plate beneath it.

Photographs and paper history (items 13, 14)

  • If the family holds the physical originals (not a printout or screen photo): front and back, the mount or album page, paper edges, and any studio stamp.
  • Any accompanying captions, envelopes, or provenance notes.

Notes on sharing & appraisals

This index and the two batch pages are set to noindex and are intentionally absent from the site's navigation and sitemap, so they will not turn up in search results. Share them by sending the direct link to a chosen recipient — an appraiser, auction house, or an Antiques Roadshow submission contact. Search-engine directives are requests to crawlers, not access control: anyone who has the URL can open it, so treat the link itself as the private thing.

For an Antiques Roadshow submission, the practical path is their official casting/submission process rather than waiting to be discovered online. Leading with the highlighted items — particularly the item-17 portrait and the item-21 candlesticks — gives an appraiser the clearest reason to look closer. Bring the objects themselves if attending an event; bring this index as supporting documentation.

Before any formal decision (estate, insurance, tax, or sale), a licensed appraiser or relevant specialist should examine the objects in person. The estimates here are triage numbers assembled from photographs and public comparables, and they will change — sometimes a great deal — once material, marks, and condition are verified.
